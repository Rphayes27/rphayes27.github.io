Blue Iguana

Blue Iguanas, also known as Grand Cayman Blue Iguanas or by their scientific name, “Cyclura lewisi,” are a species of large lizard found exclusively on the Island of Grand Cayman. With an estimated population of around 443, they are currently classified as an endangered species.

Description

Despite their names, Blue Iguanas can have skin tones ranging from grey to bright blue. The males become especially blue during mating season, while the females generally sport a shade ranging from olive-green to pale blue. Weighing over 25 pounds and reaching lengths of over 5 feet, they are the largest native land vertebrate on the Island of Grand Cayman. They are a long-lived species, with an average lifespan of 25 to 40 years in the wild, and the ability to live up to 69 years in captivity. Blue Iguanas are solitary creatures that are most active during the daytime. Like all other reptiles, they use the sun to warm their bodies, primarily inhabiting dry, rocky, and sunlit areas. They are primarily herbivores, surviving off of leaves, fruit, and flowers, though they have also been observed eating insects and fungi.

Threats

Habitat destruction is one of the primary reasons behind the Blue Iguana population’s decline. Land is consistently being cleared throughout Grand Cayman to make room for agriculture, roads, and real estate development which destroys the ecosystems these animals depend on. Other animals, such as rats, cats, and dogs, also contribute to their deaths as they have been known to attack and kill Blue Iguanas for food during various stages of their life cycle. More threats include vehicle collisions and illegal collection for international trade.

Protection

Blue Iguanas are protected under laws and international trade agreements. Some portions of their natural habitats are also protected under the National Trust for the Cayman Islands. A Blue Iguana Recovery program was also put in place to establish a successful breeding program. A head-start program is also in place which allows Iguanas to be raised in captivity and then released into protected regions once they reach a size that is less vulnerable to certain predators.
