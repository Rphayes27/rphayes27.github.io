Red Bellied Racer

Red-bellied Racers, otherwise known as Saba Racers, Orange-bellied Racers or by their scientific name, “Alsophis rufiventris,” are a species of small snake native to the Lesser Antilles in the Caribbean. They are currently listed as an endangered species, though their population trend has been steady over the last few years.

Description

Red Bellied Racers are small, brown snakes that sport black markings along their backs. They are terrestrial animals, inhabiting both forested areas as well as human-modified environments, such as towns and parks. They are primarily active from dawn until dusk, typically resting during the midday hours. Red Bellied Racers feed on small reptiles and rodents, using their weak venom to subdue their prey before swallowing it whole. Despite possessing rear fangs and venom, these snakes have gentle temperaments and are considered harmless to humans.

Threats

Red Bellied Racers face several threats throughout their remaining habitats. Natural disasters are one threat to their survival, as in 2017, Hurricanes Irma and Maria caused extensive damage throughout their native habitats which resulted in habitat loss, decreased prey populations and a spike in deaths among the snakes. Their populations are also threatened by invasive species and domesticated animals. Mongooses caused Red Bellied Racers to become locally extinct on St. Kitts and Nevis, while free-roaming cats continue to prey on the remaining populations, particularly on Saba.

Protection

Conservation groups have conducted field surveys to monitor the population of Red Bellied Racers throughout different habitats and elevations to create population estimates. These estimates can then be used as a baseline for future research and conservation efforts. Conservationists also emphasize stronger enforcement of invasive species laws as an important step in preventing these snakes from becoming locally extinct.
