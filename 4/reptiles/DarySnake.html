Dary’s Burrowing Snake

Dary’s Burrowing Snakes, otherwise known by their scientific name, “Adelphicos daryi,” are a species of snake found exclusively in Guatemala. While their exact population is unknown, they are classified as an endangered species with poor population levels that continue to decline.

Description

Dary’s Burrowing snakes are relatively large compared to other snakes within its species group, as the males often reach lengths exceeding 16 inches, while the females can get up to 22 inches in length. These snakes are native to the central highlands of Guatemala where they can be found in elevated pine-oak forests. They are terrestrial, burrowing, and fossorial animals, meaning they are well adapted to digging and spend most of their lives underground. While underground, Dary’s Burrowing Snakes tend to feed off of earthworms, slugs, and other small, burrowing invertebrates. They are also nocturnal, only emerging from their burrows and becoming active during night time. Additionally, they tend to remain in their native ranges throughout the years, rather than migrating.

Threats

Urban sprawl and habitat destruction are the main reasons behind the declining Dary’s Burrowing Snake populations. Since this species is exclusively found within the Guatemalan central highlands, any development that occurs within its limited range threatens the snakes’ population and contributes to its endangerment. By destroying the ecosystems and habitats that Dary’s Burrowing Snakes rely on, these snakes are forced from their native lands and are subject to new predators and unknown territory.

Protection

Unfortunately this species lacks a personalized protection plan and instead relies on the broader protection acts which target their native habitats and forests.
