Kagu

Kagu, otherwise known by their scientific name, “Rhynochetos jubatus,” are rare, primarily flightless birds found exclusively on the island of New Caledonia in the South Pacific. These birds are currently classified as endangered and are considered an important national symbol of New Caledonia.

Description

Kagu can be recognized by their pearl-grey feathers, bright-orange legs and bills, red eyes, and large crests. Their size is comparable to that of a chicken as they often weigh somewhere between 1.5 and 2.4 pounds, and can reach about 22 inches in length. Their average life-span ranges from 20 to 30 years. Kagu have wingspans of about 30 inches, though they are essentially flightless birds. Instead, they use their wings for balance as they move across rough terrain and to distract predators away from their young. Living most of their lives on the ground, Kagu use their strong bills to search through leaves and rocks for larvae, snails, worms, and other small insects and creatures. These birds are also monogamous, typically raising one chick each year with both parents participating in incubation and care.

Threats

Kagu have faced numerous threats originating from humans and introduced animals. During the 1800s, their crest feathers became highly sought after for decorative hats, nearly driving the species to extinction through hunting. Animals that were introduced through human activity, such as dogs, cats, pigs, and rats, also preyed upon Kagu. Habitat destruction further contributed to their population decline, with approximately 80% of their original forest habitats having been lost.

Protection

New Caledonians currently work to protect Kagu populations and their remaining habitat. San Diego Zoo Global has partnered with the government of New Caledonia to establish a DNA bank that can assist future research. Additionally, conservationists have also controlled predators within protected areas, allowing Kagu to be successfully reintroduced into a national park.
