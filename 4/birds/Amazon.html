Red Crowned Amazon

Red-crowned Amazons, otherwise known by their scientific name, “Amazona viridigenalis,” are parrots native to northeastern Mexico and southern Texas. They are classified as an endangered species with a steadily declining population of around 2000.

Description

Red-Crowned Amazons are a fairly large species of parrot, often weighing an average of about 11.2 ounces and reaching lengths of about 13 inches. Their bodies are primarily bright green, with distinctive red feathers covering their foreheads and crowns and light green feathers on their cheeks. They can live for more than 20 years in the wild, while captive Red-Crowned Amazons may survive for over 50 years. They are also social birds, living in flocks and communal roosts for much of the year. During the morning, they travel from their roosts to feeding areas. With a primary diet of seeds and fruit, these birds use both their bills and feet to manipulate their food.

Threats

Habitat destruction and illegal capture for pet trade are the primary threats facing Red-crowned Amazons. Their native habitat has experienced extensive vegetation loss and fragmentation, while thousands of wild birds were historically exported each year as pets. Poachers frequently target nestlings and may destroy the trees containing their nests while removing young birds, further reducing both the population and available nesting habitats.

Protection

Forest tracts in Mexico are being safeguarded to help protect the Red-Crowned Amazon’s native habitat, along with large private ranches and older residential trees that provide essential nesting cavities. Conservationists are also deploying man-made nesting boxes to provide more shelter and habitats for these birds. Additionally, conservationists are pushing for increased public education and regulation to help inform people of the dangers and risks of wild-caught pets and prevent further illegal collection.
