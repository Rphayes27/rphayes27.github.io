Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamander

Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamanders, otherwise known by their scientific name, “Aneides caryaensis,” are extremely rare salamanders found exclusively within the Hickory Nut Gorge of North Carolina’s Blue Ridge Mountains. Scientists estimate that only a few hundred individuals remain today, leaving the species critically endangered and at risk of extinction.

Description

Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamanders are recognisable by the brightly colored yellow markings that cover their otherwise dark, brown bodies. This appearance allows these salamanders to camouflage themselves among vegetation and rocks. Unlike many other salamander species, Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamanders spend much of their lives inhabiting trees and rocky outcrops. Their diet almost entirely consists of insects, centering around beetles and ants as their primary source of food. The entire known range of the Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamander is limited to a single 14-mile long gorge in North Carolina, which makes the species extremely vulnerable to changes within their habitat.

Threats

Habitat destruction is one of the largest threats facing the Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamander population. Development for tourism, real estate, and energy infrastructure has fragmented portions of their already limited habitats, and their small population and restricted range leave them particularly vulnerable to loss of genetic diversity through inbreeding. Natural disasters are equally as detrimental, as storms and landslides can damage and degrade their habitats even further. Climate change poses an additional threat to the species, as hotter, dryer climates can cause the salamanders to dry out and die.

Protection

The Center for Biological Diversity, Defenders of Wildlife and the Amphibian and Reptile Conservancy petitioned the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in 2022 to protect the Hickory Nut Gorge Green Salamander under the Endangered Species Act. The Center and Defenders of Wildlife have also worked alongside the North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission and other organizations within Hickory Nut Gorge to protect remaining populations. These efforts focus on obtaining stronger protections for both the salamanders and their extremely limited habitat.
