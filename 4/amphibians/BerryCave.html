Berry Cave Salamander

Berry Cave Salamanders, otherwise known by their scientific name, “Gyrinophilus gulolineatus,” are extremely rare salamanders found only within eastern Tennessee. The Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency currently lists the species as threatened within the state as its population continues to decline.

Description

Berry Cave Salamanders are long-bodied amphibians with body lengths ranging from 4 to 9 inches. Their skin tones range from salmon to pale pink with lighter bellies. Unlike many salamanders, Berry Cave Salamanders maintain some of their juvenile characteristics throughout adulthood. Even when fully grown, these salamanders display larval traits such as small eyes that lack eyelids and feathery red gills. They can be recognized by a distinctive dark stripe that can be found on the front half of their throats. As their name suggests, Berry Cave Salamanders typically inhabit caves, sinkholes, and other areas near cave entrances, particularly favoring caves with inward-flowing water. Their diets consist primarily of aquatic invertebrates such as snails and insect larvae.

Threats

Berry Cave Salamanders have extremely limited populations and are known from only a small number of cave systems throughout eastern Tennessee. Similar to most endangered amphibians, habitat degradation and groundwater contamination are the greatest threats facing these creatures. Above-ground development and urbanization have resulted in substantial population declines, as runoff interferes with the water quality and creates poor living conditions for Berry Cave Salamanders.

Protection

Researchers have conducted surveys and population studies to better understand the Berry Cave Salamanders’ distribution, abundance and conservation needs. The research is also used to guide continued conservation and management of the remaining populations, in the hopes of helping restore their numbers . Additionally, access to several caves inhabited by these salamanders is highly restricted in an effort to protect the species and its remaining habitats from disturbance.
