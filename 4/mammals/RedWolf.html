Red Wolves

Red Wolves are one of the two species of wolves living in North America. Otherwise known by their scientific name Canis Rufus, these wolves are protected as they are critically endangered, with only around 25 Red Wolves still roaming free in North Carolina.

Description

Relative to Grey Wolves, Red Wolves are recognized by the characteristic red fur found on their bodies. They sit at a weight of between 45-80 pounds and stand a little over 2 feet tall. The average lifespan for Red Wolves are between 6 and 7 years.

They are most active at dusk and dawn, and maintain primarily carnivorous diets, preying on deer and small mammals. Though, they’ve also been known to eat berries and insects. Red wolves often den in hollow trees, stream banks and sand knolls.

Like many other wolf species, Red Wolves are very social animals, using scent marking, facial expressions, body language, and vocalizations, such as howling, to communicate with other wolves. They typically reside in packs of five to eight other wolves. This pack consists of a breeding male and female, as they are known to form lifelong pair-bonds, as well as their resulting offspring.

Threats

While Red Wolves formerly roamed in many different regions such as Texas, Florida and other Midwestern states, they now reside exclusively on the Albemarle Peninsula in North Carolina.

Habitat degradation, illegal poaching and vehicle collisions are the main reasons behind these wolves’ depleted population.

Protection

There are many wildlife conservation organizations dedicated to preserving and restoring the Red Wolf population. “The Defenders of Wildlife” is one such organization.

They lead in outreach efforts to increase awareness of Red Wolves and have previously engaged in lawsuits to force the FWS (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) to protect them. Red Wolves have also been bred in captivity and released into the wild to further improve their numbers and chances of survival.
