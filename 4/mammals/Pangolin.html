Ground Pangolin

Ground Pangolins, also known as Temminick’s Pangolins or by their scientific name, Smutsia temminckii, are one of the 4 types of Pangolins found throughout Africa. While the exact population of these mammals is unknown, they are classified as a vulnerable species as their population is steadily declining.

Description

Despite their reptile-like appearance, Ground Pangolins are unique types of mammals that sport keratin scales all over their bodies. Often weighing somewhere between 11 and 42 pounds, these scales take up 20% of the Pangolins’ total body weight. They are used for protection as, when threatened, Pangolins will curl their bodies into a tight ball to expose their sharp scales to attackers. They typically live inside burrows created by other animals. They are also solitary and nocturnal creatures, staying inside until nightfall where they emerge to hunt for food. Ground Pangolins primarily feed on ants and termites which they find using their keen sense of smell, despite having poor vision and hearing. They also walk on their hind legs using their muscular forelimbs and central claws to break into insect colonies. They have long tongues similar to that of an ant-eater to collect food. Pangolins have no teeth and instead break down their food by swallowing small pieces of rock which aid in digestion.

Threats

Pangolins are the most trafficked animals in the world. They are targets for illegal hunting and poaching as their scales are highly sought after for traditional medicinal practices and their meat is considered a delicacy in certain African areas. Their skin is also used to create leather products such as purses, boots, and belts. Since a Ground Pangolin’s main defense is to curl up into a ball, they are also easy to capture and the continued demand for their scales and meat results in a steady decline in their population.

Protection

Pangolins are protected under both national and international laws, which include bans on international commercial trade. Conservation organizations also work to protect these creatures, one such organization being the African Wildlife Foundation. One way they work to decrease Pangolin trafficking is by educating consumers on the absence of true medicinal properties regarding their scales. They also work with local communities using detection dogs to locate illegally trafficked products.
