Black Rhino

Black Rhinos, otherwise known by their scientific name, “Diceros bicornis,” are one of two species of rhinoceros native to Africa. With a population of fewer than 7,000 individuals, they are currently classified as critically endangered.

Description

Black Rhinos have grey skin and are recognizable by their hooked upper lips, which they use to grab leaves and twigs while feeding. They typically weigh between 1,760 and 3,080 pounds and stand between 4.5 and 6 feet tall. Their average lifespan ranges between 40 and 50 years. Black Rhinos are primarily solitary animals and rely heavily on their strong senses of smell and hearing due to their relatively poor eyesight. They have two horns, with the front horn generally being larger than the rear horn. Their horns continuously grow throughout their lives and are used for protection, dominance, and digging.

Threats

Poaching is the greatest threat facing Black Rhinos. Their horns are made of keratin, the same material found in human hair and fingernails, but they are still highly sought after for use in traditional medicines and ornamental products. This demand has caused Black Rhinos to be illegally hunted and killed throughout their natural range.

Protection

Black Rhinos play an important ecological role within their habitats and also contribute to local economies through wildlife tourism. Conservation efforts include laws designed to prevent poaching and illegal wildlife trade, along with increased monitoring at borders and ports to prevent rhino horns from being transported and sold.
