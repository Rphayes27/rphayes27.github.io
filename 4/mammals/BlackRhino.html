Black Rhinos

Black rhinos are one of two types of African Rhinos that live in the African wild today. With a population of less than 7,000, Black Rhinos, also known by their scientific name Diceros bicornis, are a species of mammals that are critically endangered and at risk of extinction.

Description

Despite the moniker, Black Rhinos, similar to their “White Rhino” counterparts, have gray skin and are instead characterized by their pointed, or hooked, upper lips. This feature allows them to graze on leaves and twigs rather than low-growing plants.

Weighing between 1,760 to 3,080 pounds and with a height of 4.5 to 6 feet, they are rather small in comparison to other Rhinos. Their lifespan ranges from 40-50 years, and they are solitary creatures with excellent senses of smell and sharp hearing.

Additionally, Black Rhinos have two horns that grow on their heads from their skin continually throughout their lives. These horns are known to grow up to three inches every year and can get up to five feet in length. They are used to both protect their young and fight against attackers.

Threats

Despite the fact that the horns growing from Black Rhinos’ heads are entirely made of keratin, much like a fingernail, these prominent horns are also the reason Black Rhinos are critically endangered.

Their horns are highly sought after for their medicinal value in China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore, as well as for ornamental daggers in North Africa and the Middle East. Intense poaching of these animals is the main cause for their endangerment.

Protection

Rhinos are one of the oldest mammals still roaming the Earth today. They benefit their environment as well as contribute to economies by encouraging tourism.

Countries are enforcing laws to prevent poaching of Black Rhinos, implementing new technologies to strengthen border and port monitoring.
