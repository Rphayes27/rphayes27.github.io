Saola

Saola are a type of wild oxen that were first discovered in 1992 as the first large mammal discovery in 50 years. Otherwise known for their scientific name Pseudoryx nghetinhensis, Saola are often called “Asian Unicorns.” Their exact population is unknown, though it is estimated to number only in the hundreds which classifies them as critically endangered.

Description

Saola strongly represent biodiversity in places such as Lao and Vietnam where they reside exclusively in the Annamite Mountains. There is not much knowledge surrounding these creatures as they are rarely seen or documented, and all previous efforts to study them have been unsuccessful, as they often die within weeks or months of captivity.

That being said, with the information available, Saolas weigh between 176 and 220 pounds and have been documented with heights of about 33 inches. They are herbivores, feeding on leaves and plants.

Both genders have 2 recognisable parallel horns that come to a sharp point and can reach about 20 inches in length. They also have bright white marks on their faces and their muzzles contain large glands which are assumed to be used to mark territory or attract mates.

Threats

Habitat loss is the biggest threat to the Saola, though they also face population loss due to hunting and illegal trade of horns and fur. Additionally, their meat has been sold in restaurants and food markets.

Protection

These animals are extremely rare, vulnerable and culturally significant within Lao and Vietnam. They also support the environment and multiple unique types of organisms that live within the Annamite Mountains.

Areas have been designated specifically to conserve the Saola and their habitats and conservation groups continue to remove snares and traps that pose risk to these animals.
